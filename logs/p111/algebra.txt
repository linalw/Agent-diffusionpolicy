P111 algebra - does per-pad compliance centre the ring?
====================================================================================================
  the pool (logs/p110/fixture.txt): 16 fruits, d 29.6-69.6 mm, m 10-123 g
  pad-payload friction: mu_pair = (2.0 + mu_fruit)/2 = 1.25-1.46 (P104 measured mu_app 1.05-1.45)
  ring: 4 pads at 4x90 deg, one radial synergy, cap 4.90 N/pad, shipped k 1e+05
  close: 24 ticks to `wide = 1.02 d`, then 60 to the live clamp; freeze at 0.5 N for 3 ticks

1. THE CENTRING CONDITION
====================================================================================================
  Penetrations at an offset `delta` along one jaw axis:
    near (0 deg)      p = s + delta
    far  (180 deg)    p = s - delta   (contact only while delta < s)
    perp (90/270 deg) p = s - roundness
  Force = min(k*p, cap).  The payload's motion is NORMAL to the near/far
  faces (no friction there) and TANGENTIAL to the perpendicular pair, so
    drive  = near - far
    fric   = 2*mu*perp + mu_belt*m*g        (the perpendicular pair + belt)
  The payload slides while drive > fric and stops where they balance.

  TWO saturations, and P110's arithmetic conflated them:
    (i) the *near* pad saturates at cap/k of penetration - the drive yields;
    (ii) the *perpendicular pair* saturates at the same cap/k - and THAT is
         what sets the friction.  A low k helps twice: the drive is a spring
         (so it does not launch) AND the perpendicular pair is a spring (so
         its Coulomb resistance is small).

  the closed form, per regime (s = 0.70 mm on a 70 mm fruit):
    mu=1.05 k=  100000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 11.21 N)
    mu=1.05 k=   10000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 11.21 N)
    mu=1.05 k=    1000: slides to 1.68 mm                (fric  2.38 N)
    mu=1.05 k=     500: slides to 2.60 mm                (fric  1.65 N)
    mu=1.05 k=     200: slides to 5.34 mm                (fric  1.21 N)
    mu=1.05 k=     100: slides to 9.92 mm                (fric  1.06 N)

    mu=1.30 k=  100000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 13.66 N)
    mu=1.30 k=   10000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 13.66 N)
    mu=1.30 k=    1000: slides to 2.03 mm                (fric  2.73 N)
    mu=1.30 k=     500: slides to 2.94 mm                (fric  1.82 N)
    mu=1.30 k=     200: slides to 5.69 mm                (fric  1.28 N)
    mu=1.30 k=     100: slides to 10.27 mm               (fric  1.10 N)

    mu=1.45 k=  100000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 15.13 N)
    mu=1.45 k=   10000: no slide (perp pair at the cap)  (fric 15.13 N)
    mu=1.45 k=    1000: slides to 2.24 mm                (fric  2.93 N)
    mu=1.45 k=     500: slides to 3.15 mm                (fric  1.92 N)
    mu=1.45 k=     200: slides to 5.90 mm                (fric  1.32 N)
    mu=1.45 k=     100: slides to 10.48 mm               (fric  1.12 N)

  -> the STATIC fixed point (given unlimited time) is
       delta_inf = (2*mu - 1) * min(s, cap/(2*mu*k))
     The first branch (the close finishes before the friction catches the
     drive, `cap/(2 mu k) > s`) gives (2mu-1) s = 1.1-1.9 s: a 5-8x centring,
     short of the 4-contact threshold `s - 0.5/k` by the residual (2mu-2)s.
     The second branch (`cap/(2 mu k) < s`, i.e. k > 2.7e3 on this fruit)
     stops the payload EARLY, at (2mu-1)cap/(2 mu k) - and that can be
     BELOW s: the stiffness window for a static 4-contact state is
       k > (2mu-1) cap/(2 mu s) = 1.7e3 N/m.
     **So the static algebra actually favours a middling stiffness, not the
     softest one** - and the shipped k = 1e5 has the perpendicular pair at
     the cap (2*mu*cap = 12.8 N > cap = 4.9 N), so at that rung nothing
     moves at all: the rigid ring grips where it sits (P110's measurement).
     The dynamics below decides whether either branch is reachable in the
     close's 84 ticks: the launch is the term the static model cannot see.

  the same algebra for a THREE-jaw ring (120 deg), for contrast:
    drive = 1.5 k delta, friction = 2 mu k s -> delta_inf = 0.82 s  ->  CENTRES (delta_inf < s)
    so a 3-jaw chuck closes the last millimetre at this mu and a 4-jaw
    does not: the count of tangential contacts is the lever, not k.

  -> the ladder has a KNEE, not a slope.  Above k* = (cap - mu_b*m*g) /
     (2 mu s) ~ 2.2e3 N/m the perpendicular pair is at the cap, the friction
     is 2*mu*cap and nothing slides; below it the payload slides to
     (2mu-1)s + mu_b*m*g/k, i.e. 1.1-1.9 s.  So compliance converts a
     *stuck* payload into a *nearly centred* one, and every rung below the
     knee lands within 0.3-0.6 mm of the 4-contact threshold - never on it.
  -> the cap column is the ladder's negative control: lowering the cap
     lowers the friction (the perp pair leaves its cap) but also lowers the
     grip.  With cap 2.0 the knee moves to ~0.9e3, so the caps agree in
     *geometry* and differ in *capacity* (the 2-grip column halves).

3. THE GEOMETRIC BRANCH: is the perpendicular pair loaded at all?
====================================================================================================
  The aperture is `0.98 x the WIDEST horizontal extent` (the live clamp
  0.97), so a fruit whose narrow axis is `2*roundness` smaller leaves the
  perpendicular pair short by `roundness - s`:
    roundness > s  -> the pair is FREE -> the friction is the belt's alone
                      -> the ring centres, and the `[cage]` count is <= 2;
    roundness < s  -> the pair is LOADED -> section 1's friction applies
                      -> the ring jams at (2mu-1)s, off-centre.
  The pool's roundness is 0.02-0.20 d and s is 0.005-0.015 d, so most of
  the pool is in the second branch: **the registered `pads >= 3` endpoint
  and the centring mechanism are in tension** - a ring that centres on a
  round fruit cannot also read 3 pads at this squeeze, and a ring that reads
  3-4 pads is one whose perpendicular pair is jammed.

5. THE GRIP (what compliance costs the hold)
====================================================================================================
  a compliant pad's delivered force is min(k*s, cap), so a soft ring is a
  weaker hand.  The capacity that matters is P104's `n * mu * N`; the
  shipped pinch is 2*1.3*9.8 = 12.7 N.

  -> at k = 1e3 the ring's own grip is 1.8-3.6 N, i.e. **4-7x weaker than

     A pure-soft arm therefore cannot answer the *delivered* question: it
     would lose placements for a grip reason, not a centring one.  That is
     why the registration ships the **hard stop** with the soft close.

6. PREDICTIONS AND THE CHOSEN POINT
====================================================================================================
  s = 0.70 mm (1 % per face, a 70 mm fruit), delta0 = 8.8 mm
  mu_pair = 1.31 -> delta_inf = (2mu-1)s = 1.13 mm
  the 4-pad state needs delta < s - 0.5/k = 0.20 mm: MISSED by 0.93 mm

  (a) the maximum offset each k can centre (from any d0 >= the knee):
      the *terminal* offset is the bound, not the arrival - so every rung
      below the knee centres an arbitrarily large arrival to 1.1-1.9 s.
      That is the chuck behaviour, and it is what the cells' `off` measures.
  (b) the centring force, and the crush question:
      at delta = 8.8 mm, k = 1e3: drive 4.90 N vs friction 2.74 N -> it slides (the rigid ring's 4.90 N vs
      12.8 N does not).  The force is bounded by the cap by construction;
      a soft pad delivers k*s = 0.70 N at the squeeze,
      i.e. a LIGHTER touch than the shipped 4.90 N - compliance cannot
      crush, it under-grips.
  (c) the time to centre: the slide is a spring against Coulomb friction,
      i.e. a few ticks at the 120 Hz clock (the drive accelerates a 0.1 kg
      payload at ~40 m/s^2); the close's 84 ticks are not the binding term
      and the cells cannot resolve it.  What they CAN resolve is whether
      the offset shrinks at all (`off0 -> off`).
  (d) the bat, 1-D:
      k=  100000 c= 89.4 -> F@freeze  4.90 N, n=2, bat  8.80 mm, d_end  0.00 mm, freeze at 69.4 mm
      k=    1000 c=  8.9 -> F@freeze  1.89 N, n=1, bat  2.87 mm, d_end  5.93 mm, freeze at 76.1 mm
      k=     200 c=  4.0 -> F@freeze  1.11 N, n=1, bat  0.11 mm, d_end  8.69 mm, freeze at 72.7 mm

  THE CHOSEN POINT (pinned by the registration):
    k_close = 1000 N/m  (cap/k = 4.90 mm, 100x the shipped 0.049 mm)
    c_close = 8.9 Ns/m  (= 2 sqrt(k m), P104's own near-critical pair)
    cap     = 4.90 N/pad  (NOT lowered: the ladder's geometry is
              cap-free below the knee and a lower cap only lightens the grip)
    k_hold  = 100000 N/m  (the hard stop: the drive stiffens when the
              close freezes, so the carry keeps the shipped grip - a spring-
              loaded pad bottoms out, which is what a real compliant finger is)

  PREDICTIONS (each is a registered falsifier):
    P1  CENTRING: yes.  `off` falls from ~8.8 mm at the close's
        start to ~1.1 mm, i.e. the offset shrinks 7-8x.
    P2  THE COUNTED ENCLOSURE: no rise.  The residual (2mu-2)s keeps the far
        pad below the 0.5 N counting threshold, and the perpendicular pair is
        short by the fruit's roundness anyway.  Predicted 0-10 % clean.
    P3  THE BAT: falls (the force at the freeze is 0.1-1.8 N against 4.90),
        so the no-contact/ejection class improves.
    P4  THE PLACED COUNT: no rise.  With the hard stop the grip is the shipped
        ring's, so the count is P108's E (16/57 = 28 %).
    P5  NO CRUSH: the force is <= the cap by construction.

  IF P1 holds and P2/P4 do not, the phase's answer is: **compliance is a
  real self-centring mechanism, and the ring still cannot close the last
  millimetre because mu > 1 at the pads.**  The levers below the ring, in
  the order this algebra prices them: a pad material with mu < 1 (then the
  4-jaw centres to mu*s < s), a 3-jaw ring (delta_inf = 0.82 s < s), and a
  positioning change (P110's 0.6 mm, which no passive element delivers).
