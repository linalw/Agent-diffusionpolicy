  Read it as: **the delivered band is raised and the catchable window is not.**
  The pump releases every ~1.2 s of belt travel (the bed's period), so the
  approach band runs at its 10-row design depth instead of the once-per-attempt
  bursts (mean 4.2); late_dropped falls to its batch-start prime residual
  (7.9 s = the first pump's own rebase, identical on both arms). Quote the band
  with its sample type: the **same-instant census reads 5.5/6.9 -> 8.4/8.8**
  (left/right means at the selection instant), while 4.2 -> 8.3 puts 20
  boundary-pump samples against 157 continuous ones. The interval movement is
  one sample of a different branch (13/20 left 9/right 4 vs 12/20 6/6; the
  right's 22 % idle and 4.14 s lock-on are the right-arm carry class, 4 of its
  6 failures carried-not-placed) - quote the per-leg motion and the mechanism,
  not the rate delta.

  Why the catchable window does not follow - the two measured terms. The bed's
  stock is capped at **10 bodies over a 0.58 m stocked depth** (4.1 rows x 2.4
  bodies), and the slice above the moving catch's `_dynamic_select_floor()`
  (~0.40 m, against the 0.68 m release line) is **0.28 m ~= 2 rows ~= 4.8
  bodies ~= 1.8 per grade-pure lane** at the balanced A:B:C mix. So the pump
  already delivers **8.3/10 = 83 % of the band's capacity**, the right's 2.2 is
  about the aperture's ceiling, and the left's 0.9 is the grade-mix shortfall
  below it (a row is one species and the left lane takes A only). The aperture
  is a stock-depth ceiling, not a stock shortage.

  Starvation, attributed: the control's 1 is the batch-start prime (t=13.4 s,
  the belt is empty before the first boundary pump). The pump arm is starved
  **more often per slot, not less**: left **5/15 = 33.3 % steady-state**
  (**37.5 %** of all slots, 6/16) against the control's **0/10** (9.1 % of all
  slots, 1/11; Fisher two-sided ~0.057 on 5/15 vs 0/10), right 2/12 = 16.7 %
  steady-state against 0/10. The
  mechanism is a **grade drought, not a stock shortage** - at every starved
  selection instant the band is FULL and the grade-pure lane has nothing
  admitted: `band=7/10 admitted=0 window=0` t=31.50; `band=10/10 admitted=2
  window=0` t=68.46 (admitted, but all below the floor); `band=9/10 admitted=0`
  t=68.96/69.46/69.96; `band=9/10 admitted=0` t=153.94 and `band=8/10
  admitted=0` t=154.44 (right). Of the 8 starve episodes, **6 are `admitted=0`
  (the grade)**, 1 is `admitted=2 window=0` (the floor), and 1 is the
  empty-belt prime - so density does not remove the burst wait: more stock of
  the wrong grade is still the wrong grade.

  The cost, decomposed: **+20.9 % wall (484 -> 585 s) = +4.4 % sim span
  (171.8 -> 179.4 s) x +15.7 % per sim-second (2.82 -> 3.26 s wall per
  sim-second)**; the pump's own Python cost is 2.71 ms/sim-s. The single-arm
  pair is the clean form of the same fact (+30.1 % at essentially equal sim
  span, 149.7 vs 148.7 s = -0.7 %: 1.88 -> 2.47 s wall per sim-second).

  The supply burn: the pump arm's `[feed]` records **432 releases / 348 bed
  placements for 13 arm placements** (444 draws / 358 bed-placed on the
  `[bed]` line, `reached_end=320`) against the control's **126/107 for 12** -
  ~27 bed bodies per placed fruit against ~9. The pump buys the band by
  burning the pool **3.5x faster** (444 vs 126 draws), not by sorting more.

4. The verdict
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  **The mid-attempt pump does raise the delivered density, and the density is
  not what limits the line.** Measured: the delivered band **4.2 -> 8.3 / 10**
  (two-line, the 20 boundary-pump samples) with the **same-instant census
  5.5/6.9 -> 8.4/8.8** (left/right at the selection instant) and 2.6 -> 7.9
  (single arm), `late_dropped` 139.6 -> 7.9 s (the prime residual), `holds`
  0 -> 14. **Not delivered:** a higher catchable candidate count at the pick
  (two-line per arm 1.5/2.4 -> 0.9/2.2; single arm 4.6 -> 3.9), a better
  interval on a same branch, or a cheaper run (**+20.9 % two-line = +4.4 % sim
  span x +15.7 % per sim-second; +30.1 % single arm at equal span**), and the
  per-slot starvation is **worse**, not flat (left 0/10 -> 5/15 = 33.3 %
  steady-state), because the pump serves the band's depth and not the lane's
  grade. The aperture is a **stock-depth ceiling**: 10 bodies over 0.58 m, of
  which 0.28 m ~= 2 rows ~= 4.8 bodies ~= 1.8 per grade-pure lane sits above
  the ~0.40 m catch floor - the pump already delivers 8.3/10 (83 % of capacity),
  so the row pitch and the floor, not the stock depth, set the catchable slice.
  The knob therefore stays **default off** and is a measurement interface.

  The named next levers, with their measured bounds:
    - **a lower floor: CLOSED.** R3 measured the 1.5 s selection setup at
      1.467/1.516 s with 0/20 selections floor-bound (the shipped floor *is*
      the setup), and R11-A/R11-A.1 cost the shipped acceptance 9/10 -> 4/10
      (and 4/9 floored) when the band-relative floor replaced it.
    - **a higher station: bounded at <= 0.10 m.** The R10-A intercept map
      (`183_reach_intercept.py`) is the reachable band; the station pair
      separation is fixed at 0.10 m (left y=+0.000, right y=-0.100) and a
      deeper right station leaves the reach at <= 6 mm tolerance.
    - **the row composition: a drought/variance lever, not a mean lever.** The
      mean is ~1.8 eligible bodies per grade-pure lane and does not move with
      composition; what composition can remove is the *drought* (a run of rows
      of the other grade). It needs its own pre-registration, its own motion
      reference and a clearance report (the both-hands 1.9-2.6 mm class).
  More stock is not among them.
