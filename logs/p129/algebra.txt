========================================================================================================
  pool (logs/p110/fixture.txt): 16 fruits, d 29.6-69.6 mm, m 9.5-122.8 g
  ring: cap 4.90 N/pad, contact trigger 0.50 N, squeeze 0.98 (s = 1 % of d per face), close k 3300 N/m, hold k 100000 N/m
  the ladder: pair mu (0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2) ; the measured control 1.25-1.46

1. THE STATIC FIXED POINT (2-D, tangential projection; three fruit sizes)
========================================================================================================
  4 pads: drive 2 k delta (both opposed pads), resistance 2 mu k s (the
  perpendicular pair) -> delta_inf = mu s.  The far pad leaves at delta = s,
  so `delta_inf < s` is exactly `mu < 1`.  P111's (2 mu - 1) s is a different
  expression; both thresholds are 1, the residuals differ by 2x at mu 0.6.

2. THE FOUR-CONTACT CONDITION IS NOT `delta < s`, IT IS `delta <= s - 0.5/k`
========================================================================================================
  A pad counts as loaded at 0.50 N.  At the fixed point the far pad's
  penetration is `s - delta_inf`, so the four-pad state needs
     k (s - mu s) >= 0.5      ->   k >= 0.5 / (s (1 - mu))
  and the near pad must not saturate before that state is reached:
     k (s + mu s) <= cap      ->   k <= cap / (s (1 + mu))
  The two meet at **mu = (cap - 0.5)/(cap + 0.5)** - independent of s and k.

  the ceiling at the shipped cap: 4-pad mu <= 0.815
  (the 3-pad's bound is the analytic `mu < sqrt(3) = 1.732`; its two-pad
   trigger bound never binds over this pool)

  k window per fruit (N/m) - the intersection over the pool is the design answer

     P111's measured [3e+03, 4e+03] - and P111's window is a
     *three*-pad measurement.
  THREE friction conditions, and they are not the same constant:
    (i)   centres to `delta < s`                 mu < 1
    (ii)  all four pads carry >= 0.5 N          mu <= 0.815  (per fruit, cap/trigger)
    (iii) captures from an offset > s: the near pad's cap must beat the
          perpendicular pair's friction         mu < cap / (2 k s)  = 1.067-2.508 at k 3300
  and the POOL-WIDE window (one k for every size) closes earlier:
    the largest fruit's cap bound meets the smallest fruit's trigger bound at
    **mu = 0.613** (s_max/s_min = 2.35 tightens 0.815 -> 0.613)
     Below mu 0.81 the four-pad window opens; at mu 0.6 it is
     [4223, 4400] N/m over the pool (1.04x), and it **excludes**
     the shipped hold stiffness 1e+05 - the ring must be *soft while it closes*
     and firm only after (P111's hard stop, which is why that design ships the pair).

     force deficit - P104/P117's measured escape is a loaded slide at a 10-20x
     static margin - so the capacity column prices the *trade*, not an escape.

  the creep prediction (P117's correction): the *load-axis* component was
  0.00-0.07 mm on every cell at pair 1.25-1.46 and is the friction term's
  own; the total (8.62 mm on the pear) is a seat shift along the jaw axis,
  invariant to mu 2.0 -> 4.0 (P117 A6: -4.8 %).  So the prediction is:
     load-axis creep  : at mu 0.6 the capacity is still 3.1x -> stays <= 0.3 mm
     total creep      : mu-invariant (a seat term), so ~8-12 mm at every mu
  and the kill clause ('creep > 1.0 mm at the operating load') has to be read
  on the load-axis component, because the total is already 8.62 mm at the
  shipped mu - i.e. the letter of the clause fails every material including
  the incumbent.  Recorded in PREREG.md section 4 as a registered reading.

5. THE BENCH'S PREDICTIONS, IN ONE PLACE
========================================================================================================
  A0  pair friction: authoring `pad_mu = 2 mu_pair - mu_fruit` delivers the
      requested pair to <= 0.1 (PhysX averages; P104 section 7a) - a *check*,
      because the whole phase rests on the pair being controllable.
  A1  the pinch: axial break falls as `2 mu N` (13.3 -> 5.9 N from 1.36 to
      0.6), the load-axis creep stays <= 0.3 mm (3.1x the demand), and the
      total creep does not move (P117 A6, re-tested on a 2.2x ladder).
  B1  the ring, full close: mu <= 0.8 gives `centred + 4/4 loaded` from a
      20 mm arrival; mu >= 1 does not (<= 3 pads).
  B2  the ring, the task's first-contact freeze: the aperture freezes at
      `2 (R + delta0)`, i.e. `2 delta0` too wide.  At mu < 1 the payload then
      slides to the ring's centre and *unloads* (0 pads); at mu > 1 it jams
      at delta0 with the near pad carrying ~the freeze force (1-2 pads).
      Either way the line's closure does **not** enclose - the second leak
      is the close protocol, not the material.
