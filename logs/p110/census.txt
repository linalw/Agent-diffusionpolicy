2. A PERFECT LATERAL GUIDE  (dx -> 0)
================================================================================================
  the aperture need is per axis: a pad face at A/2 clears a sphere at
  offset o by A/2 - (o + r), so A = 2 (o + r_margin).
  hypothesis                              A_y (p50)      A_x  what it removes
  shipped (dy 56, dx 12)                     182.0mm    94.0mm  -
  perfect lateral guide (dx -> 0)            182.0mm    70.0mm  the lateral 12 mm only
  perfect along-belt hold (dy -> 0)           70.0mm    94.0mm  the along-belt 56 mm

  -> a guide cannot touch `A_y`: the 120 mm cage still fails 0/8 windows
     and the 200 mm cage's pass rate is unchanged (P108 section 3).
     It removes at most the *close-time* lateral (8.8 mm p50 on the cage
     cell) out of a 22.7 mm radial offset - 39 %.

  the guides the tree already has (`conveyor.containment_parts`):
    a channel between the side fences at x = 0.130 / 0.430 m
    -> 0.300 m wide against a delivered band of
       0.200-0.360 m (0.160 m): the fences
       never touch a fruit inside the band, so they cannot centre it.
       A guide that DID centre the band would have to be narrower than
       the band (0.16 m) - and the open hand (+-0.066 m at the band's
       edges) needs 0.132 m of it.

3. A PERFECT HOLD  (a metering gate: the fruit stops, the belt slips)
================================================================================================
  a stationary fruit at the station makes the entry offset the *parking*
  offset: `y_park = y_gate_face + r`, so it spans one radius across the pool
        r  y_park - pick_y
       18mm            18mm
       24mm            24mm
       30mm            30mm
       35mm            35mm
  -> A_y need falls from 182 mm to 70 mm: the
     120 mm cage (P107's own) clears, and the 200 mm hand is not needed.
  -> the 17 mm spread across the pool is the parking tolerance; the catch
     closes it at 1 mm/tick (the catch-up's own rate) - it is benign for a
     *stationary* fruit, and only a race for a moving one (P109).

  3a. THE SELECTOR CANNOT SEE A PARKED FRUIT (both pick modes)
    moving catch: `floor = _dynamic_pick_lead() + speed*select_s + margin`
      lead 179 mm + 180 mm + 30 mm
      = 389 mm - a fruit at the station is 1/20 of it
    indexed (station-first): `station` window = [0.10, 0.18] m
      (the lower edge is `pick_y - 0.02 + prepose_lead`), so a fruit parked
      at 0.00 is admitted only with `FRUIT_SMOOTH_TRANSIT_PREPOSE=0`
  -> the gate is not a scene prop: it is a collider AND a selection rule
     AND a different pick mode (`FRUIT_DYNAMIC_PICK=0`, the P1 primitive).

  3b. THE RATE ARITHMETIC (the cadence cost)
    delivered inter-arrival: 0.10-0.35 m / 0.12 m/s =
      0.83-2.92 s (mean 1.87 s)
    the pick's cycle: 15.1 s/attempt
    -> one arm consumes 1 per 15 s while the belt delivers 1 per
       1.9 s: a hold-and-release gate queues 8.1 fruit per cycle,
       1.81 m of belt - the belt is 1.60 m long, so a
       passive gate jams the line within one cycle.  A real cell answers
       this with a *magazine* (a stopped or throttled infeed), i.e. the
       belt's transport is replaced by the gate - which the tree's
       directive (the belt never stops) forbids.
    the load on the front fruit: the belt drags every queued fruit
       (mu*m*g ~ 1.3 * 0.10 * 9.81 = 1.28 N each), so a queue
       of 8 presses 10.3 N on the parked fruit - the
       fixture measures the stack directly (`fixture_gate.py`).

  3c. THE RELEASE
    the belt presses the parked fruit into the wall with ~1.3 N, so the
    lift sees mu_pair * 1.3 N of extra upward drag (~2 N at mu 1.6).  The
    grip's own capacity is 2*mu*9.8 = 12.7 N, so the bite is ~16 % - a
    real cost on the closed grip class, not a blocker.
    the *height* is the blocker: the hand's lowest solid part at the grip
    is belt_top + 80.5 mm - 14.5 mm at the
    shipped jaw height - so a gate at the station is <= 14.5 mm tall.
    Whether a kerb that short holds a 30-70 mm fruit is measured, not
    argued: the climb condition is (r - h)/r <= mu_pair, which holds on
    paper for the pool (0.19-0.59 <= 1.3) - so the fixture's job is the
    *dynamic* answer (a 0.12 m/s impact, the fruit's spin, the belt slip).

4. THE ENCLOSURE, RE-CENSUSED  (what '>= 3 pads' actually counts)
================================================================================================
  74 cage attempts across 4 cell(s)
  raw '>= 3 pads' (P107/P108's endpoint): 11/74 = 14.9 %
    - blocked descent (end > 15 mm, the plates jammed on the payload): 5
    - over-closure / crush (aper < dia - 4 mm or F > 4*cap*1.5):        4
    - CLEAN enclosure:                                                  2/74 = 2.7 %
    jam  : end p50  79.8 mm, aper p50  88.1 mm, F p50  1074.56 N, clr p50  +32.9 mm
    crush: end p50   9.4 mm, aper p50  32.5 mm, F p50   119.36 N, clr p50  +28.9 mm
    clean: end p50  10.2 mm, aper p50  63.9 mm, F p50    12.96 N, clr p50   +3.4 mm

  the modal grasp is 2 pads at the cap - a V-block, `n = 2`:
    22/74 attempts, F p50 9.74 N (1.99 cap-pairs), bat_rel p50 +58.2 mm
  -> a 2-contact V-block has `F_crit = n*mu*N` with n = 2: the SAME axial
     capacity as the shipped two-jaw pinch (P104 section 2).  The cage's
     licensed gain needs n >= 3, i.e. a centred close.

  4a. THE RING'S CLOSURE ARITHMETIC  (why a centred close is mandatory)
    one radial synergy, four force-capped position pads:
      command  a = 0.49 d (the 2 % squeeze), cap 4.90 N/pad,
      stiffness k = 1e+05 N/m -> the elastic allowance is
      cap/k = 0.049 mm of interference before the drive saturates.
    a payload offset by delta along an axis has its near pad commanded
      delta + 0.01 d INSIDE its surface, and its far pad 0.01 d - delta
      SHORT of contact.  At delta = 10 mm, d = 60 mm:
      delta= 1.0 mm -> near pad    1.6 mm inside, far pad   -0.4 mm short
      delta= 5.0 mm -> near pad    5.6 mm inside, far pad   -4.4 mm short
      delta=10.0 mm -> near pad   10.6 mm inside, far pad   -9.4 mm short
      delta=20.0 mm -> near pad   20.6 mm inside, far pad  -19.4 mm short
  -> for the far pads to reach, delta must be < 0.01 d = 0.6 mm; the
     near pads are then commanded 0.6 mm inside and push at the cap.  Any
     delta above the 0.049 mm elastic allowance is a *launch*, and the
     `bat`/`bat_rel` columns are that launch: 27-82 mm / 9-330 mm of
     payload displacement during the close (section 4's rows).
  -> so the presentation would have to centre the payload to < 1 mm,
     which no passive element on a 0.12 m/s belt does.  The term is the
     hand's closure architecture (no per-pad compliance), not the scene.
