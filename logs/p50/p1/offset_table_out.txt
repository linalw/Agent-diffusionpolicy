## E. Reproducibility

* **the 3-repeat cell** (idx 00, x=0.28, 3 fresh drops): -2.90, -2.90, -2.90 mm -> spread **0.00 mm**.
* **pass A vs pass B**, all 48 cells: max spread **0.00 mm**, mean 0.000 mm.
* **the ride** (settle -> the station edge 0.04 m, 3 instances): max x motion **0.09 mm** over 215-224 ticks - the transport is zero, as P38 measured.
* **the settle window**: the last tick with any x motion is **137** (max over 144 spawns in the s5 and s77 tables), so a 200-tick read is a safe calibration point and the 420-tick table is ~2x margin.
* **cross-process / cross-window**: the seed-77 table was measured twice in two
  processes with different settle windows (180 and 420 ticks); the per-cell
  agreement is in section M below.

## F. Is the offset independent of the spawn x?

* 13/16 instances are x-independent to <= 0.5 mm over the whole 0.16 m band; the largest three are idx 08 1.2 mm (strawberry), idx 05 13.7 mm (strawberry), idx 02 19.5 mm (kiwi).
* the x-term is **not a linear slope common to the pool**: the per-instance slope
  spans +0, -86, -93 mm/m on the three largest, against a pool median of -0 mm/m.
* so the honest model is **one constant per instance plus a bounded x-term** (<= 14 mm
  on 2 of 16 instances, ~0 on the rest), not a pooled x slope.

| strawberry | 36.3 -> +20.00, 37.2 -> -24.30 | 44.3 |

Pooled least squares `offset = a + b*dia + c*(x-0.28) + per-species intercept` over the 48 cells: residual sd **10.19 mm**, R^2 **0.744**, max |residual| **31.0 mm**, b = +0.70 mm/mm, c = -11.2 mm/m.

A (species, diameter) table therefore cannot carry the offset: two strawberries 0.9 mm
apart in diameter differ by tens of mm, because the offset is a property of the
**authored mesh** (the lateral `bias_a`/`bias_b` split and the hull bumps, drawn per
instance), not of the class. The table must be measured **per pool**.

## K. Does the offset depend on the *along* position? (ladder legality)

A ladder calibration (all 16 instances dropped at once at different along positions)
is only legal if the drop's along position does not change the offset. Four
instances (the two kiwis, the strawberry and the orange with the largest offsets),
x = 0.28, along = 0.68 / 0.50 / 0.32 / 0.14 (the last one ends past the station):

| idx | species | dia mm | off @along 0.68 / 0.50 / 0.32 / 0.14 | spread mm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 02 | kiwi | 55.8 |  +35.06 /  +35.07 /  +35.07 /  +38.37 |  3.31 |
| 05 | strawberry | 37.2 |  -24.30 /  -35.71 /  -22.01 /  -22.02 | 13.70 |
| 10 | orange | 63.6 |  -26.97 /  -26.97 /  -26.97 /  -26.99 |  0.02 |
| 13 | kiwi | 53.8 |  +53.12 /  +52.98 /  +53.43 /  +52.63 |  0.80 |

So the along position is **not** a clean factor either: the orange is flat to
0.02 mm over 0.54 m and the kiwis to <= 3.3 mm, but the strawberry's value at
along 0.50 (-35.71) is one of its *other modes* - the same value it reaches at
x = 0.36 (-35.71). That is the honest structure of this quantity:
**each instance has one or a few discrete offset modes** (a roll outcome), a
fixed initial condition reproduces its mode exactly, and the mode is set by the
release geometry (x, and to a lesser degree along) rather than by a smooth
field. A ladder calibration is therefore legal for 14/16 instances and carries
a one-mode (10-20 mm) error on the rollers; it is not a free lunch.

## L. The fresh pool (seed 6, live) - the table is a per-pool object

16 instances, 64 attributed releases, offsets **-8.4 .. +34.8 mm**; 13/16 instances repeat to <= 0.5 mm across their releases and 2/16 spread by > 2 mm (max 19.4 mm).

So a fresh seed draws a **different set of instances with a different offset
structure** (seed 5 has two +35 mm kiwis, seed 6 has one +15..+35 mm kiwi and a
+16..+22 mm apple). No shipped table can cover that: the compensation has to
measure the pool it is actually given.

## M. Predicted vs measured

* the uncompensated arithmetic above reads  0.35 % of releases below 0.180,  2.44 % below 0.20 and  0.56 % above 0.390;
  P38's census measured **0.77 %** below 0.180 and 3.5 % below 0.20 (653
  direct episodes), and P49's unique-attempt census **1.7 %** above 0.39
  (3/174). Same order, three independent populations - but all three are
  small samples of a deterministic branch, so this is a *consistency* check,
  not a rate;
* the seed-77 table was measured twice, in two processes with different settle
  windows (180 and 420 ticks): every cell agrees to <= 0.15 mm - e.g. kiwi
  idx 09 -52.82 vs -52.88, kiwi idx 14 +69.32 / +70.11 / +41.93 vs +69.41 /
  +70.20 / +41.89;
* **P38's per-class table is not the per-instance truth for the rolling class**:
  its seed-77 kiwi row reads -20.20/+15.04 while the two kiwi *instances* read
  -52.8 and +69.3/+41.9 (P38's own out-of-band list carries a kiwi at +42.0 mm and
  its text a kiwi at +69.2 mm - those are this table's +41.9 and +69.3). Its
  transit splitter restarts `x_first` mid-roll, so one 60-70 mm roll is split
  into two small ones. For the classes with small offsets (apple, pear, peach,
  lychee, one of the two oranges) the two measurements agree to **0.04-0.15 mm**,
  which is the cross-check that both probes measure the same thing.

## N. What this does not show

* **no implementation and no rate**: the compensation is phase 2; every count here
  is a measurement of the offset, and the tail shares in H are an *arithmetic
  prediction* from the measured offsets, not a run;
* **the kiwis' condition sensitivity is not attributed**: the +35/+55 mm
  bimodality of seed 5's idx 02 appears between two live releases at almost the
  same x (0.204 -> +54.6, 0.226 -> +35.0) while the isolated cell at x=0.28
  repeats to 0.00 mm. The named candidates - the wake loop's firing tick and an
  along-contact with a neighbour - are not separated here;
* **the settle window is measured on the shipped single-file schedule**: the
  patch/bed schedules put two fruit abreast 0.085-0.12 m apart, so a
  per-instance lateral shift of up to +-55 mm would change a pair's
  separation; the compensation must be scoped to the single-file schedules (or
  guarded by the pair rule) until that is measured;
* **the offset is not a proof of mechanism**: the correlation with the authored
  hull's lateral asymmetry (section G's premise) is inferred from the instance
  structure - `bias_a`/`bias_b` in `meshes.build_mesh_points` - not measured as a
  force or a contact sequence.
