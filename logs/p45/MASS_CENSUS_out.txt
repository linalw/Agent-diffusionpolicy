D. The slip ratio r = m|a+g| / (mu N) - does a lighter fruit change it?
==============================================================================
  N is GRIP-SET, not weight-set. Verified in the code:
    * the shipped dynamic grip's force comes from the finger drive's
      position target: k=1e5, c=2000 N s/m, c/k=0.02, Fsat 10 N/joint
      (control.py:218-236) against a close depth from the GEOMETRY
      (`pinch_width * 0.98`, tasks.py:9768-9771);
    * the hold servo's reference is the MEASURED tactile median capped
      at `FRUIT_DYNAMIC_FORCE_REF_MAX = 4.0 N` (tasks.py:2705-2710),
      not m*g;
    * the only two mass sites in the control path are the INDEXED /
      policy close budget `clip(m*g*100, 25, 60) N` and
      `clip(m*g*60, 20, 40) N` (tasks.py:11003, 11029; grasp.py:223) -
      not reached by the shipped dynamic line.

  The closing axis is NOT vertical: the release traces' pad-centre
  separation reads |dz|/|axis| = 0.60-0.96 (p50 0.84, n=11 holding
  traces, logs/p44/p2/amend_F_{C,S2}.log), so the payload's weight has
  an along-axis share 0.6-1.0 m g. That share is carried by a pad-to-pad
  ASYMMETRY (N1 - N2), not by more total normal force: one side gains
  what the other loses, so N1 + N2 = the drive's own preload. On the
  heaviest fruit it is 0.62-1.03 N against the 9.85 N grip (6-10 %),
  i.e. it cannot unload a pad by itself - the measured single-sided
  onsets are a SEATING property (logs/p9 section 3.1), which agrees.
  => the friction supply `mu*N` is a CONSTANT in m, so r is
     proportional to m.
     The P45 sketch's 'mass-independent if N is grip-set' is backwards:
     mass-independence is the WEIGHT-set case (N = m g / mu), i.e. the
     textbook version, which is not this cell. Halving the mass halves r.

  worst class = apple: m = 104.9 g, mu_lo = 0.45
  pinch N = 9.85 N (P41 shipped) / 4.42 N (P40 record)

  |a| =  1.00 m/s^2   (the commanded lift cap (a_max 1.0))
     scale    m [g]  demand [N]   r @N=9.85   r @N=4.42  1/r @9.85

  |a| =  5.83 m/s^2   (the measured worst single-tick transient (P40))
     scale    m [g]  demand [N]   r @N=9.85   r @N=4.42  1/r @9.85

  VECTOR note: |a - g| for a horizontal transient is sqrt(a^2+g^2), not
  a+g. At 5.83 m/s^2 that is 11.41 m/s^2 (r 0.270 at 1x) against P40's
  scalar-sum 15.64 (r 0.370). P40's number is the upper bound;
  the tangential form (g_share 0.54, from the measured pad axis) reads
  r = 0.263 at 1x. Every form gives the
  same 2x / 4x scaling - only the intercept moves.

  What the record says about whether that improvement is worth anything:
  `logs/p9/FRICTION_AUDIT.md` measures the real ratio per leg - escapes
  0.10-0.78 of the available friction on 29/31 legs (median 0.36), HELD
  legs median 0.37, i.e. the ratio does not separate the classes. A 0.25x
  mass moves a quantity that is already non-discriminative.

E. The fling v = J/m - the same impulse, a lighter fruit
==============================================================================
  Anchor: `logs/473_probe_final.log` trial 16 - a 2.8 cm lychee released
  by the fast SYMMETRIC relax at peak |v| = 1.335 m/s; the SHIPPED
  asymmetric relax on the same pool/offsets (`logs/474_probe_asym.log`,
  trial 16) reads 0.695 m/s, and that run's max 0.748 m/s is the
  <= 0.75 m/s convention.

  the 2.8 cm lychee: m = 8.01 g at rho 850
  the impulse that produced 1.335 m/s: J = 10.70 mN s

     scale    m [g]   v same J   vs 0.75   same J on a 6.6cm apple
      1.00     8.01       1.33      1.8x                  0.102 m/s
      0.50     4.01       2.67      3.6x                  0.102 m/s
      0.25     2.00       5.34      7.1x                  0.102 m/s

  the same J = 10.7 mN s is 101.9 mm/s on a
  6.6 cm apple (104.9 g): the ejection speed is a pure 1/m
  statement about the SAME contact state, which is why the light fruit
  is the one that flies. 0.5x doubles it, 0.25x quadruples it.

  A second, independent 1/m channel: at grip-set N the friction force
  mu*N is fixed, so the roll-out angular acceleration
  alpha = tau/I = mu*N*R / ((2/5) m R^2) = 2.5*mu*N/(m*R) scales as 1/m.
  For the 6.6 cm apple that is 3.2e3 rad/s^2 at 1x and 2x/4x
  that at 0.5x/0.25x - the walk, if it is the rotational mode, gets
  FASTER as the mass falls. The two channels point opposite ways.

==============================================================================
E2. The contact / rolling term - does mass enter the geometric walk?
==============================================================================
  The walk's own geometry carries NO mass term. All four sites are pure
  length algebra:
    * `pinch_width(sample, quat)` = the mesh's extent along the closing
      axis (`axis_extent`, tasks.py:12529-12546) - a shape profile, no
      mass;
    * the close depth `pinch_width * 0.98` (tasks.py:9768-9771);
    * the carry's commanded span and the pads' local-width shrink: the
      condition is that the fruit's projected width between the faces
      falls as the hand advances - kinematics of the two rigid bodies;
    * the release relax target `pinch_width + PAD_THICK + margin`
      (tasks.py:12151-12172) - geometry plus a 6 mm margin.
  `grep 'sample.mass' src/fruit_sorting/tasks.py` finds only the two
  INDEXED close budgets (11003, 11029). Nothing in the dynamic carry,
  the servo, or the release reads a mass.

  So the walk is a kinematic/contact-state path, and the mass enters it
  only through the two 1/m channels already computed:
    (i)  the friction FORCE is mu*N (grip-set), so the roll-out angular
         acceleration is 2.5*mu*N/(m R) - worse as m falls;
    (ii) a depenetration kick is an impulse, so its speed is J/m - worse
         as m falls (section E).
  What a lighter fruit DOES improve is the translational force margin r
  (section D) - the exact quantity `logs/p9` measures as
  non-discriminative (0.36 escapes vs 0.37 held).

  Prediction the PREREG can state and falsify: a mass scale should move
  NOTHING in the timing (the profiles are tick-clocked by caps, not by
  inertia: logs/p44/CAP_CENSUS.md rule 1) and nothing in the walk; only
  the fling and any force-limited leg can move. If a 0.25x arm changes
  the `[cycle]` block times, the mass is leaking into a path this
  census did not find.

F. What a 0.25x mass implies physically (fixed size)
==============================================================================
  Two equivalent readings of the same number: the DESIGN density rho
  (what `fruit_density` returns) and the SOLID-EQUIVALENT density
  m/V_sphere (what a physicist would call the density, since the 0.82
  factor is only a shape fudge).

    fruit                d   m ship  rho ship  rho 0.5x  rho 0.25x  solid 0.25x
    peach 6.5cm        6.5     96.7       820       410        205          168
    kiwi 5.6cm         5.6     67.9       900       450        225          184
    apple 6.6cm        6.6    104.9       850       425        212          174
    orange 6.5cm       6.5    106.1       900       450        225          184

  Reference densities [kg/m^3]: water 1000, real peach ~1000 (the P11
  audit's own 137 g at 60 mm implies 1215), real kiwi ~1050, cork ~240,
  balsa ~160, pine ~500. So:
    0.5x  -> 410-450 kg/m^3 = softwood; floats; not fruit.
    0.25x -> 205-225 kg/m^3 = cork; floats high; definitely not fruit.

  The one physical way to read it: a HOLLOW SHELL of real-density flesh.
    0.50x peach 6.5 cm: m =  48.3 g -> a wall of 4.15 mm at 1000 kg/m^3
    0.25x peach 6.5 cm: m =  24.2 g -> a wall of 1.93 mm at 1000 kg/m^3
    0.50x kiwi 5.6 cm: m =  33.9 g -> a wall of 3.98 mm at 1000 kg/m^3
    0.25x kiwi 5.6 cm: m =  17.0 g -> a wall of 1.84 mm at 1000 kg/m^3

  And the physical version of the SAME experiment: at the real density,
  0.25x the mass is a SMALLER fruit -
    peach 6.5 cm -> 4.1 cm
    kiwi 5.6 cm -> 3.5 cm
  - which changes the pinch span (`pinch_width`), the pad standoff and
  the reach: a different geometric experiment, not a mass one.
